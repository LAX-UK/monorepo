Row click: page or panel

What happens when someone clicks a row in a list, in Bid admin, LAX Admin and the client and organisation workspace. Every list was checked in the code and in the running apps with the seeded development data.

The problem

In both staff tools most lists open a side panel that repeats what the row already shows, plus an ID, and then sends people on with a button to the full page: two clicks and a wait to reach the work. The panel also breaks the habits people bring from the web: Cmd-click opens the list again instead of the record, Back does not close the panel, and the Orders row does one thing on its number and another everywhere else.

The rule

Six recommendations. They refine the existing line in the staff design standard (“Side panel or full page”, PRD-7 and TBL-8) rather than replace it.

R1

A row opens the record’s full page Recommended

Click, tap or Enter on a row goes straight to the page. The list is one step behind it, and Back returns to it exactly as it was.

Why: 22 of the 42 panels today repeat the row and hand over to a page that already exists, or have one action that points somewhere else, so the panel only adds a click.

Shopify Polaris: index table rows open the resource details page. Carbon: data table rows link to the item.

R2

Keep a panel only for work queues Recommended

A panel stays where staff decide on many rows one after another and the decision fits in the panel: fulfilment, payout runs, holds, requests, AML, manual review. The panel shows the facts needed to decide, the actions (money actions confirmed), its position (“3 of 12”) and moves to the next record with J and K.

Why: in a queue, leaving the list costs context on every row; elsewhere the panel is just a smaller page.

Linear: peek and triage view for inbox work. Notion: side peek, with “Open as page”.

R3

Every row is a real link Recommended

The title cell is an <a> to the record page, never to “the list with the panel open”. Cmd- or Ctrl-click, middle-click and Cmd+Enter on the row open the page in a new tab; right-click offers Copy link.

Why: staff open five records in tabs to compare them; today that gives five copies of the list.

Our TBL-8 already asks for this. Primer and Carbon data tables keep the primary cell a link.

R4

Back puts you where you were Recommended

View, search, filters, sort and page live in the URL on every list, and the scroll position and the row you opened are restored. Opening a queue panel adds a history entry, so Back (and the phone’s back gesture) closes it.

Why: sort is lost on 12 Bid admin lists today, and Back currently leaves the list instead of closing the panel.

Shopify admin and Linear keep list state in the URL; GOV.UK service manual: never break the Back button.

R5

Every panel has an address and a way out Recommended

A panel’s URL reopens it even when the record is not on the current page. Where a page exists, the header says “Open full page” (the same words everywhere). Where the record is small and has no page (an audit event, an invitation, a stock hold) the panel is the record and its URL is the link to share.

Why: today six panel lists have no URL, the way out has more than ten names (“Open order”, “Open review”, “Open full record”, “Edit artwork”…), and a shared link to a record that is not on the current page opens nothing.

Notion: every peek has a link and “Open as page”. Carbon side panel: for supplementary detail, not the whole task.

R6

One rule in all three places Recommended

Bid admin, LAX Admin and the client workspace behave the same, through one shared list component. The client workspace already does: every row there is a link and no row opens a drawer. The rule goes into the staff design standard and the existing list-template lint check.

Why: staff move between the two admins every day; muscle memory should carry over.

Our NAV rules already ask for one behaviour across both staff tools.

Behaviour details

Back restores the list

  • View, search, filters, sort and page or cursor are in the URL on every list, including the 12 that sort locally today.
  • The scroll position is saved when a row is opened and restored on Back; the row you opened is focused and briefly highlighted.
  • A record page opened from a list shows Previous and Next through that list (the kit’s RecordPager), with the list query kept.

New tab

  • The title cell is a link to the page. A click anywhere on the row follows it.
  • Cmd- or Ctrl-click, middle-click and Cmd+Enter open the page in a new tab; Shift-click keeps selecting a range.
  • Queue panels use the same link, so a new tab opens the page, not the list.

Keyboard

  • J and K (or arrows) move between rows; Enter opens; Cmd+Enter opens in a new tab; X selects.
  • In a queue panel, J and K move to the next and previous record without closing it.
  • Esc closes the panel and returns focus to the row; on a page, Back (or Alt+←) returns to the list.

Phone

  • A card tap opens the page. The back gesture returns to the list where you were.
  • Queue panels open full screen with a sticky footer for the actions, and Back closes them.
  • Cards stay links, so long-press offers “Open in new tab”.

The page while it loads

  • The page header renders at once with the title and status from the row, so the click feels instant; sections show skeletons of their final height.
  • Target: the header within 100ms, the page usable within one second on the seeded data.

Empty, missing and failed

  • Record gone: “This order no longer exists” with a link back to the list, keeping its filters.
  • Load failed: the header stays, the failed section shows an error with Try again; nothing else is lost.
  • No access: the page opens read-only with a notice saying who can change it.

Open questions for the owner

1. Should payments and Bid payouts get their own full pages?

Neither has a page today; their panels hold capture, refund, mark paid, adjust and retry. A page would give them a timeline and a link to share with finance.

Recommended default: keep the improved panel now; build a payment page and a payout page later as one large piece of work, after #1349 lands.

2. Where should a LAX Admin staff row go?

Staff are read-only in LAX Admin and managed in Bid admin.

Recommended default: no panel; show roles in the row and link the name to the Bid admin staff page, marked as opening Bid admin.

3. On a phone, should work queues open pages too?

The panel is already full screen on a phone, so the difference is only the address and the stepping.

Recommended default: keep the queue panel on phones as a full-screen view with its own URL; Back closes it.

4. LAX Admin enquiries: queue panel or conversation page?

Staff triage enquiries (assign, close) but reply on the conversation page. Bid admin enquiries already open the page.

Recommended default: open the conversation page, as Bid admin does; assign and close stay in the row menu.
