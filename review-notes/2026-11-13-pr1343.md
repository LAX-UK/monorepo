- **Custom bands** (20/15/12.5 with edges at £100k and £500k): the slices are summed exactly in ten-thousandths of a penny and rounded once. Band input in any order is sorted first. A first band that does not start at 0, or thresholds that are not ascending, throw.

## Findings

### H1 (High): lots that are live or sold but not yet invoiced in sales that already have bands get repriced to their stored 25%, with no way for staff to fix them

The migration leaves every existing lot's `buyer_premium_rate` in place (usually the old default, 0.2500). The resolver now treats that stored value as a staff override that wins over the sale's bands. Before this PR, the sale's bands always won and the lot rate was never read for a banded sale.

The PR notes say this affects only *unsold* lots, and that staff fix those with the bulk action. It also hits two groups the notes do not mention:

1. **Active lots (bidding open) in a banded sale.** They change from bands to 25% at deploy, in the middle of bidding. Bidders already bid under the terms the lot page showed. The bulk action skips them (`not_editable`), and `lot-update.ts` refuses premium edits on active lots. The overview callout even says they "keep their premium", which is true only in the sense that they keep the wrong one.
2. **Ended, sold lots in a banded sale with no invoice raised yet.** `InvoiceRaiser.price` prices them at raise time through the new resolver. A £600,000 hammer:
   - was going to be charged £60,000 (the old 10% on the whole hammer);
   - is now charged £150,000 (25%);
   - would be £85,000 under the clause 11 bands.

   Nothing lets staff repair these: the bulk action refuses them and so does lot edit. `verify-buyer-premium-config.ts` only looks at draft, scheduled and active sales, so they never appear in its output.

Reproduced with `resolveBuyerPremium({ lotRate: "0.2500", sale: <standard bands> })`. It returns `source: "lot"` and a premium of 15,000,000p on a 60,000,000p hammer.

This goes beyond the approved scope. The scope keeps sold and invoiced lots as they are and fixes unsold lots through the bulk action. It does not approve repricing lots that are live or already sold.

**Fix first. Any one of these:**
- **(a) Prove there are zero affected rows in production before deploy:** lots in a sale with non-empty `buyer_premium_tiers`, status active, or ended with a winner but no live `bid_invoice`. Extend the verify script to ended sales and to "sold, not invoiced", and gate the deploy on it.
- **(b) Owner sign-off to NULL the lot rate in the migration** for lots whose sale has non-empty bands. This preserves behaviour exactly, because the old code never read those rates. It does change rows, hence the sign-off.
- **(c) Have the resolver or `InvoiceRaiser` ignore a legacy rate** on a lot that was already active or ended in a banded sale at deploy. This is the hardest of the three.

Whichever you choose, add an integration test that seeds an active lot and a sold, uninvoiced lot at 0.2500 in a banded sale.

### M1 (Medium): public "sold for" figures for already-invoiced lots are recomputed under the new rules, not read from the invoice

These surfaces recompute the premium from the live rules on every read:
- the lot detail (`catalog-lot-read-http-application.service.ts` → `computeLotCheckoutPricing` → `soldForWithPremium`);
- sale results (`sale-results-summary.service.ts`);
- hang labels;
- quick look and cards (through `checkoutPricing`).

Only the buyer's own dashboard swaps in the stored invoice (`user-dashboard-read.service.ts`). That covers just the winner, never the public.

For a lot in a banded sale that has already been invoiced, these surfaces now show a figure the buyer was not charged:
- 25% from the H1 legacy rate, or
- the new slice maths, where the invoice was raised with one rate on the whole hammer.

This breaks the requirement that historical invoices, receipts and sold lots still show the rate actually charged. The PR's "Sold lots show what was actually charged" is true only for the winner's view.

**Fix:** for sold lots with a live invoice, price the public and results surfaces from the stored invoice lines (as `checkoutPricingFromInvoice` already does), or at least the results totals and the lot's sold line.

### M2 (Medium): bulk reset reads lot status outside its transaction

`useSalePremiumForLots` loads the lots and checks their status, then opens the transaction and updates by id without checking again.

A scheduled lot that opens for bidding between the read and the write gets its premium cleared while live. The window is small, but this is exactly the case the guard exists for.

**Fix:** put the status check in the UPDATE (`WHERE status IN ('draft','scheduled') AND winner_id IS NULL AND buyer_premium_rate IS NOT NULL`), or re-read the lots with `FOR UPDATE` inside the transaction.

Also, the transactional path (`transactionRunner` plus `withTx`) is not exercised by any test. The integration test passes `transactionRunner: null`.

### L1 (Low): "10% above" can be read as 10% on the whole hammer

The buyer and staff copy (`describeBuyerPremium`) reads "15% up to £500,000, 10% above". That is the old, wrong reading. Clause 11 says "10% on any balance above £500,000".

The invoice line and the worked example are unambiguous; the headline terms on the lot page, quick look, sale page and lot form are not. Suggest "15% up to £500,000, then 10% on the part above". This is copy only and needs no Conditions change.

### L2 (Low): `/admin/lots/:id/metrics` still formats the lot's own rate

`admin-lot-detail-metrics.service.ts` still builds `buyerPremiumLabel` from `lot.buyerPremiumRate`. It returns `null` for lots that use the sale's premium and "25%" for legacy lots, bypassing the resolver. The PR says the web no longer reads it.

Remove the field, or route it through `resolveBuyerPremium`, so a future consumer cannot show the wrong rate.

### L3 (Low): the runbook is stale

`docs/runbooks/buyer-premium-tiers.md` (the precedence table and code sample) and `docs/runbooks/dashboard-audit.md` still describe `buildBuyerPremiumPolicy({saleTiers, lotRate})`, `TieredBuyerPremiumPolicy` and "tiers override the lot rate".

### L4 (Low): the worked example is not announced, and disappears silently

The sale form's worked example changes live without an `aria-live`/`role="status"` region, so screen-reader users are not told the new figure.

When the custom bands are invalid, the example notice disappears with no replacement text. Suggest a polite live region and a "Fix the bands to see an example" state.

### L5 (Low): ATG cannot take new sales unless staff choose a flat rate

New sales now default to the standard bands. `assertSaleMappableForAtg` rejects any sale with bands ("remove the bands"), so every new sale needs staff to pick Flat before it can sync to ATG.

That rejection is expected, because ATG takes one rate. But a flat 15% sent to ATG would understate nothing and overstate above £500k, which differs from clause 11. Worth a line in the sale form for ATG sales and an owner decision on what ATG lots should charge.

### I1 (Info): invoice line shows a slice that charged nothing

At £500,000.01 the invoice line reads "Buyer's premium (15% of £500,000 + 10% of £0.01)", although that slice adds £0.00. The figure is correct; the wording is only slightly odd.

### I2 (Info): the rollback is safe

`0375_rollback.sql` fills only NULLs before `SET NOT NULL`, so the rollback cannot fail on NULL rows. This was verified against Postgres 16 with the PR's integration test: migrate, then roll back, then migrate again.

Lots that use a banded sale get the sale's flat rate. Under the old code that rate is unused, because tiers still win there. The NOT NULL scan runs under `lock_timeout = 5s`, so run the rollback in a quiet window.

### I3 (Info): an invalid sale rate charges nothing

A sale flat rate that is not a valid rate (for example `"15"`) charges £0 with no signal. The DB `numeric(5,4)` column and the input validator make this unlikely, but a warning log would make it visible.

### Verdict: FIX FIRST

The maths, rounding, resolver coverage, bulk-action auth and audit, and the tests are solid. H1 can reprice live and sold-but-not-invoiced lots in sales that already have bands, and staff have no remedy. M1 shows the public figures that were not actually charged for historical banded lots. Resolve H1 (at minimum, option (a): a production query showing zero affected rows, plus a verify script that covers ended sales) and M1 before merge. M2 and L1 can follow, but are cheap.

### Findings

#### H2 (High): an invoiced lot in a banded sale keeps its legacy 25%. After a buyer default, the re-offer and re-raise charge 25% instead of the bands.

The approved migration clears only lots with no live invoice, so invoiced lots in banded sales keep `buyer_premium_rate = 0.2500`. The old code never read that value. Under the new resolver it is a staff override.

When the buyer defaults, `drizzle-buyer-default.repository.ts` voids the invoice ("so the lot can be invoiced afresh"). From then on there is no live invoice to read, and every fresh pricing uses the legacy rate:
- `create-reoffer-services.ts` `priceFor` → `InvoiceRaiser.price` → `buildInvoiceLines`. This prices the offer the underbidder sees and accepts, and the invoice raised for them.
- `reoffer-seller-proceeds.ts` uses the same pricing for the seller's figure.
- `reoffer-reads.service.ts` `premiumOf` uses it for the defaulting buyer's `claimableShortfall.premiumLostMinor`.

**Reproduced on Postgres 16:**
1. A banded sale has a sold lot at 0.2500 with a live invoice.
2. Migrate. The lot keeps 0.2500, which is correct under the approved rule.
3. Void the invoice.
4. `buildInvoiceLines` on the row gives `flat:0.2500` and a premium of **£150,000 on a £600,000 hammer**. The bands give £85,000. The old code gave bands too.

Staff cannot repair it: the lot is `ended`, so lot edit refuses it and the bulk reset skips it.

The pre-deploy check lists the lot as "invoiced: shows and charges its stored invoice" and passes. It is flagged (`sold_not_invoiced`) only if someone re-runs the check after the void.

**Fix first. Any one of these:**
- **(a)** With owner sign-off, extend the migration to clear the rate on invoiced lots in banded sales too. Their invoices are frozen and the public surfaces read the invoice (M1), so nothing visible changes. This goes beyond the approved rule ("NULLs lot rates in banded sales that have no invoice"), hence the sign-off.
- **(b)** Make `--pre-deploy` count invoiced lots in banded sales that hold a rate differing from the bands. Gate the deploy on zero, or on an owner-accepted list.
- **(c)** Price a lot whose last invoice was voided under the schedule its sale gives, ignoring a rate that predates 0379.

Add an integration test: buyer default on such a lot, then the re-offer price and the shortfall.

#### M3 (Medium): the pre-deploy gate exits 1 on normal data, so it cannot be used as a gate

In `verify-buyer-premium-config.ts`, the "⚠ own premium differs" check uses the stored lot rate (`lotPremiumDiffersFromSale(lot.buyerPremiumRate, sale)`), even with `--pre-deploy`. Before the migration, every draft or scheduled lot in a banded sale still holds its old 0.2500. Each one is marked ⚠ and counted, and the script exits 1.

Reproduced: the output reads "No live or sold-but-not-invoiced lot changes premium." The ⚠ lines themselves show "£85,000 (sale_bands)", yet the exit code is 1. Every production run will fail, so the gate that backs H1 will be overridden by habit.

**Fix:** when `--pre-deploy` is set, use the post-migration rate (`lotRateAfterMigration0379`) for the ⚠ check too. Move the exit-status decision into the unit-tested lib, and add a case for it.

#### L6 (Low): a sale with no bands and blank lots is not called out

After deploy, a sale with no bands charges its flat rate on the whole hammer. A sale row written outside the app gets the 15% default, which is £90,000 on £600,000 against £85,000 under clause 11.

The verify script says "Every unsold lot charges its sale's premium" and flags neither the sale nor its unopened blank lots. It does flag a blank active lot, as "✖ live: premium changes at deploy". That reason is wrong after deploy, though the lot does block (exit 1). This is allowed by the approved rule ("else sale flat rate"), so it is Low.

**Suggestion:** print a per-sale "no bands: charges a flat N% on the whole hammer" notice, and word the blank-lot case as "no legacy rate", not "changes at deploy".

#### L7 (Low): legacy single-line invoices are not overlaid (M1 residual)

`findInvoicedSalePrices` skips invoices whose only line is `legacy_total` (`legacy_backfill`). Their lots fall back to today's rules on public and results surfaces. In a banded sale that is the kept 0.2500 (H2), so the public "sold for" can show 25%.

**Suggestion:** show the legacy total as the sold-for figure, or count these lots in the pre-deploy check.

#### L8 (Low): the lot-page premium wording for a sold, invoiced lot comes from the resolver, not the invoice

`checkoutPricingFromInvoicedSale` replaces the figures but drops `premium`, and `lotBuyerPremiumCopy` resolves the wording from the live rules. A legacy invoiced lot in a banded sale can read "Buyer's premium: 25%" beside a figure taken from a banded invoice.

#### I4 (Info): hammers above about £90 trillion lose precision

`current_price` is `numeric(18,2)`, up to 10^16 pence. The resolver goes through `Number` above 2^53, so the precision loss is unreachable in practice.

#### I5 (Info): the staff KPI revenue series ignores lot overrides

`admin-sale-overview-kpi-trend.service.ts` prices the daily revenue series on the sale's terms only. It is a staff estimate and does not charge anything.

#### I6 (Info): stale migration number in two commit messages

`85064eb6` and `d59f7f93` say "migration 0375". The code is correct (0379).

#### I1 (Info): still present

At £500,000.01 the invoice line reads "15% of £500,000 + 10% of £0.01". The figure is correct.
