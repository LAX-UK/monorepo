### F1: fixed

- **Where:**
  - `cumulativeVat` (`src/money.ts:158`) is `round_half_up(lineVat × credited / lineGross)` in BigInt.
  - It returns exactly `lineVat` once the line is fully credited.
  - `nextCredit` (`src/document.ts:354`) takes that figure less what earlier credits already took.
- **Round-1 cases now** (the PR's own test):

  | Credits | VAT credited | Net credited |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 3p + 1,197p | 200p | 1,000p |
  | 501p + 699p | 200p | 1,000p |
  | 9p + 1,191p | 200p | 1,000p |
  | 400 × 3p | 200p | 1,000p |

- **My probe:** 3,000 random invoices, each with:
  - two standard goods lines (quantity up to 3);
  - one margin-scheme goods line;
  - two standard delivery lines.

  Each invoice took random sequences of goods and delivery credits of 0p to 100,000p, finished with a final credit, and then had one more 1p credit attempted. That made 12,095 credit notes. Credited gross and VAT were tracked for each invoice line on its own.
- **Results:**
  - **0** cases where a line's credited VAT went over the line's VAT. The worst case was exactly equal.
  - **0** cases where a line's credited gross went over the line's gross.
  - 28,497 checks of a fully credited line: VAT was exactly the line's VAT every time.
  - Every finished sequence summed to exactly the invoice's net, VAT and gross.
  - The extra 1p was always refused.
  - Margin-scheme credit lines always carried 0 VAT.
  - Every credit line was `tax_inclusive`.
- **Can `Math.min(vat, take)` (`document.ts`, `nextCredit`) clamp and lose VAT?** Not for any invoice line `buildLine` can make: the line's VAT-to-gross ratio is at most 1/4 (net 3p, VAT 1p), so a step's VAT never goes over its gross. It is a safe guard, not a live path.

### F4: fixed for rewritten priors

- **Where:** `replayPriorCredits` (`src/document.ts:380`) rebuilds every prior credit line through `nextCredit`, on the same ledger, in order. It must reproduce the description, treatment, net and VAT exactly, and the prior's totals must add up.
- **Probe:** the round-1 attack and its variants are refused with `bad_invoice`:
  - the prior emptied (`lines: []`, totals 0);
  - the prior rewritten (VAT 1, net 1,199);
  - the prior's lines doubled.
- **Priors passed in a different order** are refused when the rebuild differs (183 of 300 random cases). Where they are accepted, the result is the same as the original order. This is fail-closed.
- **Leaving a prior out** is not covered. That is F7.

## New findings

### F7. Low: a prior credit note that is left out is not detected, and its VAT can be credited again

- **Where:** `src/document.ts:443-467`. `buildCreditNoteFor` trusts that `priorCredits` is complete. Nothing compares the priors with `buckets.refundedGoodsPence` / `refundedDeliveryPence`, although those come from the PSP and are passed in the same call.
- **Probe:** an invoice of one standard line, net 1,000p and VAT 200p.
  1. Credit A is 3p, with 1p of VAT.
  2. Credit B is 1,197p. With A passed as a prior, B has 199p of VAT. With A left out (buckets still say 3p was refunded), B is **accepted** with 200p of VAT.
  3. Total VAT credited: **201p** on a 200p invoice.
- **Bound:** gross is still capped by `planRefund` (the real paid and refunded buckets). The VAT error is about 1p per rounding step that is counted twice, so about 1p per omitted credit line.
- **Impact:** it needs the runtime to load priors incompletely, for example a credit note still `pending` or `sending` that was left out of the query. That is a plausible bug in the runtime PR. The error would then go into the VAT return, and no totals check would catch it.
- **Fix (follow-up, does not block this PR):**
  - In `buildCreditNoteFor`, refuse (`bad_invoice`) when the summed prior gross by role is not equal to `buckets.refunded{Goods,Delivery}Pence`. If refunds and credit notes are not always 1:1, refuse at least when it is lower.
  - Add a test that leaves one prior out.
  - Until then, add a line to the README's "owned by the runtime" paragraph: priors must include every credit note for the invoice in every state except `cancelled` and `failed`, in creation order.

## What is good

- `cumulativeVat` is the right rule. It is monotonic, it uses integer arithmetic (BigInt for products above 2^53), and it gives an exact finish. Because each step is the difference of two cumulative figures, no sequence can drift.
- Replaying priors through the same `nextCredit` means there is one rule. There is no second checker that could drift from the builder.
- The property test covers what round 1 asked for: sequences that stay within the line and then meet it exactly. It ends with a refused extra 1p.
- The basis is now part of the data, not only the README. #1306 already enforces it when storing.
