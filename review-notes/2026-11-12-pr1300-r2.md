## Previous High findings

### H1. References collide across products: **Fixed**

- **References are product-scoped.** `laxReference(product, kind, sourceId)` produces `LAX-<SHOP|ART|BID>-<KIND>-<id>` (`src/document.ts:77-86`).
  - Source ids are lower case only, 1–64 characters (`:63`), which covers L5.
  - `parseLaxReference` inverts it (`:92-100`).
  - The prefixes are `[A-Z]+` and the id is lower case, so the split is unambiguous.
  - Probe: `("shop","invoice","123")` gives `LAX-SHOP-INV-123`, and `("lax_art",…)` gives `LAX-ART-INV-123`. Upper case `ABC` is refused with `bad_source_id`.
- **Builders cannot take a reference string.** They take `sourceId` and derive the reference. Contact, invoice and allocation references are checked for kind *and* product (`checkRef`, `:198-209`). Probe: a Shop invoice pointing at `LAX-ART-CON-c1` is refused with `bad_reference`.
- **Lookups are product-scoped.** `DocumentLookupPort.findByReference(product, kind, reference)` (`src/ports.ts:66`).
- **Test added:** an injectivity and round-trip property test (`src/document.test.ts:62`).

### H2. Provider totals are not compared: **Fixed**

- Both `issued` and `found` outcomes now carry `ProviderDocument.totals` (`src/ports.ts`).
- `completeSending(…, expected)` and `applyLookup(…, expected)` route both outcomes through `acceptProviderDocument` (`src/lifecycle.ts:174-192`). A mismatch becomes `needs_review` with `lookup:"clear"`, the provider identity kept, and `mismatch` recorded.
- `requeue` and `cancel` refuse a record that already has a provider document.
- Probe: a `found` result one penny over gives `needs_review`, with `mismatch {field:"grossPence", differencePence:1}`.
- `expectedTotals(document)` (`src/document.ts`) gives the caller the right figure for every kind.

### H3. Builders accept forged lines and references: **Fixed**

- `Line` carries a type-only brand. `buildInvoice` and `buildBill` take `LineInput[]` and call `buildLine` themselves (`buildLines`, `src/document.ts:210-222`).
- References are derived, not passed in.
- Dates are real calendar dates (`src/dates.ts`).
- The `bad_reference` code is used now.
- Probe: the round-1 literal (negative net, payment reference, 2026-02-30, 2026-13-45) is now refused.
- Residual risk: see N4.

### H4. A second create is possible after `unknown` then `not_found`: **Fixed**

- **Settle time is kept on unsettled records.** `unknown` and `needs_review/owed` carry `settlesAtMs = lease.expiresAtMs + SETTLE_GRACE_MS` (60 s) (`src/lifecycle.ts` `toUnknown`).
- **Early `not_found` is refused.** `applyLookup` takes `nowMs` and refuses `not_found` with `lookup_too_early` until `nowMs >= settlesAtMs`.
- **The lease length is capped.** `MAX_LEASE_MS` caps it at 1 h.
- **Documented.** The README says the HTTP timeout must be shorter than the lease.
- **Probe:** lease to 100 000, unknown at 31 000, then `settlesAtMs` 160 000:
  - `not_found` at 120 000 is refused with `lookup_too_early`;
  - `not_found` at 160 000 gives `pending`.
- **Test added:** a stateful property test, "random action sequences never send twice without a settled not_found between" (`src/lifecycle.test.ts:371`).

## New findings

### N1. Medium: a partial credit on a standard-rated line is refused for one gross amount in six

- **Where:** `src/document.ts:288-297` (`netForGross`) and `:364`, `:375` (`not_representable`), inside `buildCreditNoteFor` (`:314`).
- **Scenario:**
  1. A Shop invoice has a standard-rated goods line, with net £1,000.00 and gross £1,200.00.
  2. The customer is refunded £10.03 of goods. `planRefund` accepts it.
  3. `buildCreditNoteFor` refuses it with `not_representable`. With integer-penny VAT at 20% half-up, net *n* gives gross `n + round(n/5)`, so every gross ≡ 3 (mod 6) pence has no net that produces it.
  4. The probe over 1–6000 p refused exactly 1000 amounts (3, 9, 15, 21, 27, 33 p, …). Margin-scheme and zero-rated lines are unaffected. Full-line credits are unaffected (they reuse the line's net).
- **Impact:** if the runtime moves the refund through the PSP before it builds the credit note, the money has left with no document in Books. Books and the bank then disagree until a person intervenes.
- **Fix (one of the following):**
  - Make credit lines tax-inclusive: VAT = `gross − round_half_up(gross × 10000 / 12000)`, with the line marked inclusive so the provider's totals still match exactly. Zoho Books supports inclusive tax per document.
  - Or expose the representability check (e.g. `checkCreditable(invoice, split, priorCredits)`) and require it before money moves. State that order in the README.
  - Either way, add a property test: every split accepted by `planRefund` is creditable, or is refused *before* the refund.

### N2. Low: malformed provider totals throw or produce a record that cannot be read back

- **Where:** `src/lifecycle.ts:174-192` (`acceptProviderDocument`) and `src/money.ts:151` (`compareWithProviderTotals`).
- **Scenario (probe):**
  - An adapter returns `found` / `issued` without `totals`. Then `applyLookup` throws `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'netPence')` instead of returning a `Result`.
  - The totals contain `NaN`. Then the record becomes `needs_review` with `differencePence: NaN`. That is stored as JSON `null`, and `parseRecord` (`:360`, `parseMismatch`) later refuses it as `bad_record`, so the row can no longer be loaded.
- **Impact:** fail-closed (nothing is issued twice), but the record gets stuck.
- **Fix:** in `acceptProviderDocument`, require `isPence` on all three provider fields. On bad input, return a `needs_review` (`lookup:"owed"` if a settle time is known) or a `bad_outcome` refusal. Test both cases.

### N3. Low: `parseRecord` does not tie the reference to the kind, or re-check the lease cap

- **Where:** `src/lifecycle.ts:365` (only a non-empty string is required) and `:378` (any `isTime` expiry is accepted).
- **Scenario (probe):**
  - `{kind:"invoice", reference:"LAX-SHOP-PAY-1"}` parses OK.
  - `{reference:"anything"}` parses OK.
  - A `sending` row with `expiresAtMs: 9e15` parses OK. That lease never expires, so the record is never looked up. This is the M1 effect, reintroduced through storage.
- **Fix:**
  - Require `parseLaxReference(reference)` to succeed, with `kind` equal to the record's kind (and the product, if the record stores one).
  - Optionally bound `expiresAtMs` to a sanity window, or let the runtime expire it with its own clock.

### N4. Low: `buildCreditNoteFor` trusts the `Invoice` object it is given

- **Where:** `src/document.ts:314` onward. `Invoice` is a plain interface, and `Line`'s brand survives an object spread.
- **Scenario (probe):**
  1. Take `{...invoice, lines: invoice.lines.map(l => ({...l, unitNetPence: 10_000_000, vatPence: 0}))}`. It type-checks without a cast.
  2. Pass it, with matching buckets, to `buildCreditNoteFor`. The result is a £50,000 credit note.
- **Why it matters:** the runtime must rehydrate an invoice from storage days later to credit it. There is no `parseInvoice`, so the natural code is `row as Invoice`, which is the H3 gap moved to credit notes.
- **Fix (one of the following):**
  - Rebuild the invoice's lines through `buildLine` inside `buildCreditNoteFor`, and check that `invoice.totals` equals `totalsOf(lines)` and that `invoice.reference` parses as this product's invoice.
  - Or provide `parseInvoice(row)`, or brand `Invoice` as `Line` is branded.

### N5. Low / Info

- **Contact email:** `buildContact` does not validate the email. Probe: `"not-an-email"` is accepted. The provider will reject it, so the record goes to `failed`. Validate a minimal shape, or document that the adapter does.
- **Payment allocations:** a `payment` is compared on amount only (`expectedTotals`). A provider that auto-applies the payment to different invoices would still "match". Have the runtime/adapter compare allocations, or extend `ProviderDocument` for payments.
- **Expected totals:** `expected` totals are supplied by the caller and are not stored on the record. The runtime must pass `expectedTotals(document)` for the *same* document. Consider carrying the expected totals (or a hash of them) on the record.
- **Ambiguous lookups:** `ambiguous` with `count: 0` is accepted. This is harmless; it ends in `needs_review`.

## Design notes

- The per-status union, the settle time and the stateful property test make never-create-twice hold structurally, not just by convention. Every illegal transition I tried is still refused. Escalating from `pending` or `sending` is refused. `rejected` → `failed` → `requeue` is allowed and documented, so the adapter must map only definitive 4xx responses to `rejected`.
- The purity guard and layer rule keep the package free of clocks, randomness and I/O.
