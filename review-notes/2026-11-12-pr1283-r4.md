## Claimed-step semantics (checked in code and in the simulation)
- **A closed window claims the step, so it's never sent late.** `stageForRecipientsDue` returns early, the caller calls `claim(id, step)` (insert-if-absent), and the step drops out of `paymentReminderOpen`/`dealReminderOpen`. Outage scenario E: 634,488 steps were claimed unsent and **0** were sent late or on the wrong day.
- **It never blocks a later step.** Claims are per `(id, step)`. `reminderStepOpen` moves on to the next step when that step's London day opens, and the `count(*) < 3` filter still admits the row. In scenarios C, D and E every later step still reached every recipient: `blockedLater` = **0**.
- **Collection reminders (claim-first path) and `paymentReminderToSend` don't claim a closed step.** They just return `null`, and the row ages out of the query after 4 days. That's harmless: no send, and no effect on later steps.
- `reminded` counts only real sends. Claim rows aren't read anywhere outside the two stores, so a "claimed unsent" row is never shown as "reminder sent".
- `now` is taken once per job run. With ≥ 45 min of slack for lot invoices (see below), a slow 200-row batch can't push a staging past the close in practice.

## Nothing double-sends
- Outbox keys are still per recipient and step: `payment_reminder:<payment>:<step>:<recipient>` and `private_sale_<kind>_reminder:<deal>:<step>:<recipient>`. Re-staging is a no-op, and claims are `onConflictDoNothing`.
- Brute force: no recipient ever got two steps on the same London day, in any scenario (`twoSameDay` = 0). Overlapping or parallel runs only re-stage the same keys.
- The lot and deal services run in the same 15-minute job, sequentially (`create-buyer-default-services.ts:93`).

### N-2 (Low): the 4-day tail is 96 h, which is shorter than the `overdue` window across the autumn clock change for deals due in the first hour of a London day
- **Where:** `drizzle-private-sale-deal-reminder.store.ts` (`LAST_STEP_TAIL_DAYS = 4`, `gt(due, now() − make_interval(days => 4))`). The same constant is in the payment store, but invoice due times are end-of-day, so lots aren't affected.
- **Why:** a deal deadline is `now + N days` (`private-sale-deal/transitions.ts:181,191`), so it can be any time of day. Take a deadline of 00:05 London on D, with the last Sunday of October between D and D+3. `due + 96 h` is then 23:05 London on D+3. The `overdue` window runs to 23:59 London on D+3. A recipient at UTC+10 (Brisbane, Vladivostok, Guam, Port Moresby, …) is scheduled at 23:00 UTC. Any poll after 23:05 no longer lists the deal.
- **Brute force:** on time, 0 misses. With a 14-minute lag, or with that single poll missed, **54** `overdue` misses each (9 zones × deadlines at 00:05 on 3 October days per year in 2026–27). These are misses only, never late sends.
- **Fix:** set `LAST_STEP_TAIL_DAYS = 5` in both stores. The send-time guard already rules out late sends, so a looser query bound is safe.

### N-3 (Low, perf): the new bounds are not served by any index
`EXPLAIN` with `enable_seqscan = off` on PG 16, using the SQL captured from the built stores:
- **Lots:** the plan is driven by `lot_status_end_time_idx (status = 'ended')`, then `payment_buyer_id_status_idx`. `created_at > now() − 90 d` and the new `due_at` range are applied as **filters**. `bid_invoice_due_at_idx` is partial (`voided_at is null`) and sits on the nullable side of a LEFT JOIN inside an OR, so it can't be used. As a result, the cost still grows with every ended lot ever, and the poll now runs 4× as often as on main.
- **Deals:** `private_sale_deal_open_idx` (partial on stage, keyed on `updated_at`) is scanned, and `stage`/`payment_due_at`/`collection_due_at` are filters. There's no index on either deadline column.
- The commit message says the bounds use "plain column comparisons so indexes stay usable". They're sargable, but no index matches them.
- **Fix (follow-up is fine):** add partial indexes `private_sale_deal (payment_due_at) WHERE stage = 'invoiced'` and `(collection_due_at) WHERE stage = 'paid'`, plus `payment (created_at) WHERE status IN ('pending','cancelled')`. Today's table sizes don't make this urgent.

### N-4 (Info): deal deadlines near London midnight have a `due_day` window shorter than the stated one-missed-poll tolerance
A deal due at 00:05 (or 00:20) London has a `due_day` window of 5 (or 20) minutes. Every recipient is scheduled at London midnight. With the poller on time it is sent (0 misses). With a 14-minute lag or one missed poll the window closes first, so the step is claimed unsent: **610,280** `due_day` cases in 2026–27 have under 30 minutes of slack, all of them from these two deadline times. This is the correct trade-off under the plan (never after the due time; main would have sent at 09:00, after the deadline). If the product wants a reliable `due_day` email for deals, snap deal deadlines to the end of the London day, as `invoiceDueAt` does for lots. That would also remove N-2.

## Brute-force results
**Script:** a Node simulation of `PaymentReminderService`/`DealReminderService` plus `stageForRecipientsDue`, using the built `@auction/domain` (`reminderOpensAt`, `reminderClosesAt`, `reminderSendAt`). It models the candidate query bounds (`due ≤ now + 72 h`, `due > now − 96 h`, `count < 3`; UTC session), the open step, the closed-window claim, per-recipient `reminderStepReached`, outbox-key dedupe, and claim-when-all-staged.
- **Recipients:** each case has two, the zone and a no-zone/London payer.
- **Zones:** all 418 `Intl.supportedValuesOf("timeZone")`.
- **Days:** every step's London day from 2026-01-01 to 2027-12-31.
- **Lot invoices:** due at the end of the London day.
- **Deals:** due at 00:05, 00:20, 00:40, 06:00, 12:34, 23:10 and 23:55 London.
- **Polls:** event-driven over BullMQ-aligned polls (:00/:15/:30/:45 UTC). On 7 zones (lot and deal) this was cross-checked against a full every-15-minutes poll loop and produced identical tables. The cheap mirror was also checked against the real `reminderStepOpen`/`reminderStepReached`/`reminderWindowClosed` on about 4,900 sampled cases × every poll: 0 mismatches.
- **Scenarios:** A on time. B every poll 14 min late. C one missed poll (the poll that would send step *s* to recipient *r* is skipped; run for each *s*, *r*). D two consecutive missed polls. E (lots only) a 6-hour outage starting at that poll.
- **Case counts:** C/D/E count once per (step, dropped recipient), so they're twice A.
