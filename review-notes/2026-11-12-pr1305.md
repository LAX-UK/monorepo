- **F1 (High).** Splitting one line's credit over several credit notes can credit more VAT than the invoice charged. Two credits that add up to the full £12.00 line credit £2.01 of VAT on a £2.00 invoice VAT. Four hundred 3p credits credit £4.00 of VAT.

The package is still not wired into any runtime, so no money moves today. F1 is a money-correctness rule that totals checks will not catch, so it needs settling here, in the pure domain, before the runtime PR is built on top of it.

## Findings

### F1. High: partial credits on one line can credit more VAT than the invoice charged

- **Where:** `src/money.ts:131-146` (`buildInclusiveLine`) and `src/document.ts:396-415` (the loop in `buildCreditNoteFor`).
- **Cause:** each credit line's VAT is `round_half_up(gross / 6)` of *that credit's* gross. Nothing ties the sum of the credits on a line to the line's VAT. Prior credits are counted by gross only (`grossByRole`), never by VAT.
- **Probe:** invoice of one standard-rated line, net 1,000p, VAT 200p, gross 1,200p. Each case is two credit notes that add up to the whole line:

  | Credits | VAT credited | Net credited | Invoice VAT |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 3p + 1,197p | 201p | 999p | 200p |
  | 501p + 699p | 201p | 999p | 200p |
  | 9p + 1,191p | 201p | 999p | 200p |
  | 400 × 3p | **400p** | 800p | 200p |

- **Impact:**
  - LAX would reclaim output VAT it never charged. The error is 1p per affected pair, and unbounded for many small credits.
  - Net sales are under-credited by the same amount.
  - The provider totals check does not catch it. If the provider computes inclusive VAT the same way, it agrees with us, so this goes straight into the VAT return.
  - "Totals exact" is met per document but not per invoice.
- **Fix:** allocate VAT cumulatively per invoice line.
  - VAT on a credit = `round_half_up(lineVat × (priorGrossOnLine + take) / lineGross) − priorVatOnLine`, using integer arithmetic.
  - The credit that exhausts a line then takes exactly the rest of the line's VAT and net.
  - `priorVatOnLine` comes from the prior credit notes' lines, mapped to invoice lines in the same "in order" way the gross already is.
  - Add a property test: for any sequence of accepted partial credits on an invoice, the summed credit VAT is never more than the invoice's VAT per line, and equals it exactly once the line is fully credited.
- **Interaction with F2:** a provider that recomputes inclusive VAT per line (`round(g/6)`) cannot reproduce a cumulative figure. Before the runtime PR, confirm in the Zoho Books sandbox that the adapter can make Zoho record an exact VAT amount on a credit-note line. If it cannot, the domain must refuse, **before the refund moves money**, a split whose cumulative VAT would differ (this was option 2 of N1: expose `checkCreditable(invoice, split, priorCredits)` and require it before the PSP call). Then add the property test N1 asked for.

### F2. Medium: credit lines are tax-inclusive, but nothing tells the adapter

- **Where:** `src/money.ts:131-146`. `Line` (`src/money.ts:62-70`) and `CreditNote` have no inclusive flag. The README sentence at `README.md:57` is the only mention.
- **Cause:** a credit `Line` stores `unitNetPence = gross − vat` and `vatPence = vat`. It has the same shape as an exclusive line, but `vatPence` is no longer `vatForLine(lineNet)`.
- **Probe:** for gross 1–1,200p on a standard line, recomputing VAT from the stored net (the exclusive rule every other line follows) differs on **200 of 1,200** amounts. Examples:
  - 3p becomes net 2, VAT 1; exclusive VAT is 0.
  - 9p becomes net 7, VAT 2; exclusive VAT is 1.
  - 15p becomes net 12, VAT 3; exclusive VAT is 2.

  These are exactly the "≡ 3 (mod 6)" amounts from N1.
- **Impact:** an adapter that sends `unitNetPence` with the standard tax code gets a provider gross 1p lower. The record ends in `needs_review` *after* the refund has moved, and a credit note with the wrong amount now exists in Books. Before this PR, the same amounts were refused up front. That was safer.
- **Fix:**
  - Put the mode on the document, e.g. `CreditNote.amountsAre: "tax_inclusive"`, or a per-line `grossPence` with an `inclusive: true` brand.
  - Have `expectedTotals` and the README adapter contract say that the adapter must send the gross with inclusive tax.
  - Add a test that recomputes provider-side under the declared mode and gets exact totals for every gross.
  - Also document that `Line.vatPence === vatForLine(lineNet)` no longer holds for credit lines. PR #1306 relies on that invariant (F3).

### F3. Medium (merge order): breaks PR #1306 once both are on main

- **Where:**
  - `src/lifecycle.ts:371`: `parseRecord(row, nowMs)` now takes a second, required parameter.
  - On `books2-accounting-tables`, `packages/persistence/src/lib/accounting-document-row.ts:125` calls `parseRecord(recordInput(row))`.
  - On the same branch, `packages/persistence/src/lib/accounting-document-payload.ts:36` requires every stored line's VAT to equal `buildLine`'s exclusive VAT.
- **Probe:**
  1. A scratch merge of this branch into `origin/books2-accounting-tables` fails `pnpm -r build` with `accounting-document-row.ts(125,10): error TS2554: Expected 2 arguments, but got 1`.
  2. With that call patched locally, `readDocumentPayload` accepts a 6p credit note. It refuses 3p and 501p credit notes with "a line's VAT differs from the rule". So the registry would refuse most partial credit notes, and none could be read back.
- **Fix:**
  - Agree a merge order. Whichever PR lands second updates the persistence reader: pass the clock's `now()` into `parseRecord`, and validate credit-note lines with the inclusive rule (or with whatever F1/F2 settle on) instead of `buildLine`.
  - Better: export a `parseDocument(row)` from the domain, so storage stops re-implementing line rules.

### F4. Low: prior credit notes are still trusted

- **Where:** `src/document.ts:372-385`.
- **Cause:** the check only requires that a prior credit's lines add up to its own stated gross. It does not rebuild them or compare them with anything stored.
- **Probe:** a real full credit (1,200p), passed back as `{...credit, lines: [], totals: {0,0,0}}`, is accepted as a prior. The next full credit of 1,200p is then accepted (with paid buckets of 2,400p), so 2,400p is credited against a 1,200p invoice.
- **Mitigation:** `planRefund` caps the credit at what the PSP really paid and refunded, so this needs both inputs to be wrong.
- **Fix:** take prior credits through the same parser as storage (see F3), or check the sum of prior credits against a stored per-invoice figure.

### F5. Low: `parseRecord`'s lease bound has no allowance for clock skew

- **Where:** `src/lifecycle.ts:376` and `:396-398`.
- **Scenario:**
  1. Worker A takes a lease of `MAX_LEASE_MS` (1 h).
  2. Worker B's clock is 2 s behind A's. B reads the row, and `expiresAtMs > nowB + MAX_LEASE_MS`, so `parseRecord` refuses it as `bad_record`.
  3. That row cannot be loaded, expired or completed until B's clock catches up. `unknown` and `needs_review` settle times have the same edge.
- **Fix:** allow a small skew in the bounds (e.g. `+ 60_000`), or cap `leaseMs` in the queue below `MAX_LEASE_MS`. Add a test at the exact bound.

### F6. Info

- `isTotals` accepts negative totals. A provider reporting negative pence ends in `needs_review` (mismatch), which is safe.
- The README N3 paragraph (line 87) is one very long line, unlike the rest of the file. This is cosmetic.

## What is good

- The N2 refusal is typed, leaves the record untouched and is tested for both entry points. Every record produced reads back.
- `checkInvoice` is the right shape for N4. It rebuilds through the same constructors, rather than adding a second rule set.
- `parseRecord` now ties the reference to the kind and refuses the 9e15 lease from round 2.
- Full-line credits are unchanged. For a whole line, the inclusive split gives the same net and VAT as the original exclusive line. Probe: all 1,200 single full credits match; proof: `round((n + round(n/5)) / 6) = round(n/5)`.
